Battery ETA

Hours left and when to charge, for your iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Mac.
SupportPrivacy

A percentage doesn't tell you whether you'll make it to tonight. Battery ETA learns how fast each of your devices drains at every hour of your day and turns that into time left and a "charge by" time.

Learns your day

Heavy afternoons and quiet nights are forecast as exactly that. Estimates sharpen over the first few days, and Low Power Mode learns its own model.

Every device together

iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Mac share readings through your own iCloud, so each one shows the others.

Mac: at this pace

On a Mac it learns what each app adds to the power draw, so a local AI model and a word processor forecast differently, and shows which apps are worth quitting.

Widgets, complications and alerts

Home Screen, Lock Screen, StandBy, desktop widgets and watch complications, plus notifications when something's running low or won't last until tomorrow.

Support

Why does it say "Calibrating" or "Learning"?

Each device needs a little time on battery before it can estimate. Until its model finishes training, the forecast blends in a typical rate for that kind of device.

A device is missing

Open Battery ETA once on that device. iPhone, iPad and Mac sync through iCloud, so all of them need to be signed in to the same Apple Account with iCloud Drive turned on. The watch syncs through its paired iPhone.

I don't see it in the Mac menu bar

If the menu bar is full, macOS hides items behind the camera notch. Remove or rearrange items (hold ⌘ and drag), or choose Settings → Mac → Menu bar shows → Icon only. Opening Battery ETA from Spotlight always shows its window.

Why are app costs on the Mac missing?

An app needs about an hour of use, with and without it open, before its cost can be separated from the rest. Apps that are always open can't be told apart from the Mac's own draw.
